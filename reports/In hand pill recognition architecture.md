# Locate on device, identify with gated cloud agreement

The most reliable and practical architecture for My Pharmacist splits the work three ways. The phone does everything spatial: quality gating, auto-capture, finding each pill, cropping it and drawing the red highlight. The phone also runs a cheap embedding check against the patient's own enrolled reference photos. OpenAI then labels each numbered crop, but only from a closed list: the patient's registered medication IDs plus "none" and "unknown". A pill turns green only when the on-device match, the OpenAI label and an imprint check written in code all agree on a medication that is due now. Anything else is "cannot confirm" or red. This design follows from three findings in the evidence. First, no general-purpose recognizer is reliable enough to confirm a pill on its own: ChatGPT-4 got 22 of 25 clean pill photos right, and every miss was a misread imprint that produced a plausible wrong drug or strength ([PMC11391868](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11391868/)). Second, OpenAI's own documentation says its models struggle with precise spatial localization ([OpenAI Vision guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)), so the per-pill red highlight has to come from the device. Third, shrinking the problem to the patient's few medications helps enormously (99.5% vs 80.1% detection mAP@50 at 3 vs 32 classes ([MEDISEG](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10825))), so the real danger moves to a pill that is *not* in the set being forced onto its nearest match. Open-set rejection therefore matters more than raw accuracy. Cost is not a constraint: at prices read on 30 Oct 2026, one scan costs about $0.007–0.018 on the mid-tier GPT-6.1 models, so $80 covers thousands of scans. The binding constraints are localization of pills on skin, which no public dataset covers, and an honest false-confirmation evaluation. The concrete first experiment below tests both in about a week for a few dollars.

## The literature says "likely match", never "confirmed"

There is no benchmark for this exact task: about four pills in a palm, checked against three to eight known medications, with a reject option. The nearest evidence comes from harder problems, so it gives a floor rather than an estimate. The 2016 NLM Pill Image Recognition Challenge matched consumer photos against 1,000 unseen pill types. The winner reached only **MAP 0.27 and about 43% top-5**. Embossed or debossed pills scored about 34% top-5, and white round or oval pills, which made up 36% of the test set, scored MAP of only about 0.08–0.09 ([Yaniv et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5973812/)). ePillID, the largest low-shot benchmark, is the strongest argument for metric learning against reference images. With one reference per appearance class, multi-head metric learning reached **92% MAP@1 when both sides of the pill were used, but only about 76% with one side**. Plain classifiers reached about 57%. All models still failed on shiny pills and on look-alikes that differ by a single imprint character ([ePillID](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.14288)). A palm scan shows one face per pill, so the one-sided figure is the relevant one.

Capture conditions matter as much as the model. In a 19-drug study that crossed background, flash and exposure, the best top-1 was **51.1%** (white background, flash on, EV +2.0), and accuracy moved sharply with every factor ([Cha et al., JMIR Med Inform 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8367115/)). No study measures palm against flat-surface capture. A skin-coloured, creased, shadowed background is plausibly worse than a plain tray, so this is the first assumption the team should test. Prescription context is the most encouraging lever. On VAIPE, the only public dataset with **in-hand, multi-pill smartphone photos**, adding prescription knowledge raised macro-F1 from 0.52 to 0.86 ([PIKA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.02432)) and detection mAP by about 6 points ([PGPNet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10538799/)). Imprint-aware pipelines reach about **78% top-1 on consumer photos with one training image per pill** ([Heo et al., JMIR 2023](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e41043)).

Two conclusions follow. First, no published system, commercial or academic, has shown it can confirm a pill without a human check. A 2021 systematic review found almost no accuracy studies of pill-ID apps ([PLOS ONE review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7861418/)). The app's language should be "matches your registered X". Uncertain results should fall back to "check the bottle or ask your pharmacist". Second, a closed set of a few medications makes confusion *within* the set fairly rare. That leaves three failure modes to design against: a pill outside the set (an old prescription, another person's pill, a vitamin, a mint); a different strength of a registered drug; and an imprint misread.

## False confirmation is prevented by AND-gated rejection, not by confidence scores

The open-set literature points to a simple design. Vaze et al. showed that a strong closed-set model is the main driver of good "none of the above" rejection, and that specialised open-set methods add little on top of it ([arXiv 2110.06207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06207)). With tiny per-patient sets that change whenever a prescription changes, retraining a classifier is the wrong shape. The better pattern is retrieval: embed each pill crop and compare it with every enrolled reference image. Let *d1* be the distance to the best class and *d2* the distance to the best *other* class. Confirm the best class only if (a) *d1* is below an absolute threshold, which rejects distractors, and (b) *d1/d2* is below a ratio threshold, Lowe's test, which rejects ambiguous look-alikes ([Lowe 2004](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~lowe/papers/ijcv04.pdf); [Sun et al., kNN OOD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06507)). Choose thresholds by **target false-accept rate on a calibration split that includes look-alikes and distractors**, not by accuracy. Selective classification provides the formal framing, the risk–coverage curve ([Geifman & El-Yaniv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)). Conformal prediction gives a clean rule: confirm when the set size is 1, otherwise report uncertain. Its guarantee holds only on average across calibration draws, and the tutorial suggests n of about 1,000 for tight behaviour, far more than a student team will collect ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). Treat it as a principled way to pick thresholds, not as a guarantee.

Never use the model's own confidence as the gate. Text LLMs mostly report 80–100% confidence regardless of correctness, and checking consistency across repeated samples works better ([Xiong et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)). Vision-language models are likewise notably miscalibrated when they state confidence in words ([Xuan et al., EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2025.emnlp-main.74.pdf)). Agreement between models helps only as far as their errors are independent. Large LLMs share errors heavily: when two are both wrong, they give the same wrong answer about 60% of the time, even across providers ([Kim et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)). So the two recognizers should use *different cues*. One is an on-device appearance embedding (colour, shape, size). The other is a reader focused on the imprint (OpenAI's `imprint_text`, then a string comparison in code against the registered imprint, which is the check that would have caught all three ChatGPT-4 errors ([PMC11391868](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11391868/))). Combine them as an AND gate: any disagreement means uncertain. This can only lower false confirmations, at a measurable cost in abstentions. Temperature scaling and ensembles matter only if the team trains a classifier, and even then calibration can fail under mild shift such as a new phone or new lighting ([Ovadia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)).

Enrolment is the other lever. A 5-way 1-shot result on CURE of **60.78%** ([BRACIS 2025](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/bracis/article/view/40905)) shows that single-reference enrolment is unsafe for confirmation. Enrol several shots per side for each medication, under varied light, on a palm and on a plain surface. Keep all of them and match against the nearest, rather than averaging them into one prototype. About 5–10 shots per side is an assumption to test, not a number from the literature.

## OpenAI identifies crops well enough and cheaply, but must not localize or decide

All current OpenAI models accept images. On the official pages read on 30 Oct 2026, the recommended lineup was `gpt-6-astra` ($10 / $50 per 1M input/output tokens), `gpt-6.1-sol` ($2 / $10), `gpt-6.1-terra` ($1 / $5) and `gpt-6-luna` ($0.10 / $0.50) ([OpenAI Models](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models); [OpenAI Pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). **These names and prices were read through a summarizing fetch, so recheck them on the live pages before budgeting.** On patch-based models, a high-detail image costs about 1.2 tokens per 32 px patch, capped at 2,500 patches, so about 3,000 tokens ([Vision guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)). The notes' estimates for the recommended request (four 384 px crops, four labelled reference crops and the medication list, about 5.2K input and 0.8K output tokens) are below.

| Model (rates read 30 Oct 2026) | Est. cost per scan | Scans per $80 |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-6.1-terra | ~$0.009 | ~8,700 |
| gpt-6.1-sol | ~$0.018 | ~4,300 |
| gpt-6-astra | ~$0.092 | ~870 |
| gpt-6-luna | <$0.001 | >80,000 |

The tokenizer table does not list Sol, Terra or Luna, so these estimates assume they behave like their siblings. Confirm with `usage.input_tokens` on one real call. Reasoning tokens are billed as output and could add $0.02–0.05 per scan at high effort. In practice, development iteration and runaway retries will use up the budget before demos do, so set a hard project spend limit. Batch and Flex cost half as much and suit offline re-scoring of the test set. Batch data is not eligible for zero retention and is kept until deleted ([Your Data](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)), so never send real photos through Batch.

Three capability facts shape the design. **OpenAI documents no bounding-box output and says its models struggle with precise spatial localization and give approximate counts** ([Vision guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)). A box that lands on the wrong pill is a safety error, so OpenAI should never place the red highlight. Instead, the phone sends crops with stable IDs (`pill_1`…`pill_4`) and the model labels IDs only. Strict JSON-schema Structured Outputs are supported from GPT-4o onward. All fields must be required, optional fields are written as nullable, and on base models numeric ranges cannot be enforced, so validate `confidence` in client code ([Structured Outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)). Build `matched_medication_id` as an enum per request: the patient's IDs plus `none` and `unknown`. Ask for `imprint_text: null` when the imprint is unreadable rather than a guess, and handle `refusal` and incomplete responses as uncertain. Few-shot reference images are cheap at about 173 tokens per 384 px crop. Evidence for them is mixed: they helped GPT-4V on pathology ([Ferber et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07407)), but VLMs often fail at recognition tasks defined only by examples ([Doveh et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12736v1)). That makes them an A/B variable, not a default.

The alternatives are each better at one thing. Gemini officially returns detection boxes (`box_2d`, normalized 0–1000) and polygon masks under a JSON schema, at comparable prices (`gemini-3.8-flash` $0.75 / $3.75, rising on 1 Jan 2027) ([Gemini Image Understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding); [Gemini Pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). That makes Gemini a credible fallback if on-device localization fails. Claude documents pixel-coordinate workflows ([Claude Coordinates guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision-coordinates)). Neither has published pill evidence, and no head-to-head of GPT, Gemini and Claude on pill identification exists. That gap is a legitimate contribution the CS497 report could make. A backend proxy makes the provider a server-side setting, so the app does not commit to one.

### Three non-negotiable integration rules

The **API key must not ship in the app**. OpenAI's guidance is to keep keys out of mobile clients, route requests through a backend, and enforce spend limits, because alerts alone do not stop traffic ([OpenAI key safety](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/5112595-best-practices-for-api-key-safety)). A small serverless proxy (Cloudflare Worker, Vercel or Firebase) should hold the key, add a shared token or App Attest check, apply a per-device daily cap and a body cap of about 2 MB, force `store: false`, and forward the request. The iOS side is then a plain `URLSession` POST with `Codable` decoding, with no SDK and a small Swift 6 concurrency surface. On **data retention**, OpenAI does not train on API data by default. Responses API objects are stored for 30 days unless `store: false` is set, and **abuse-monitoring logs keep inputs for up to 30 days even with `store: false`**. Zero Data Retention requires sales approval ([Your Data](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). The privacy statement must therefore say "the app does not keep photos", not "photos are never retained anywhere". On the **photo policy**: send images inline as base64, never through the Files API. Strip EXIF and downscale before upload. Never touch `PHPhotoLibrary`. Hold pixels only in memory, or in a temporary file deleted when the scan ends. With mock data and no real patients, PIPEDA and Ontario's PHIPA likely do not bind the prototype. The notes did not verify this, and a real deployment would need a privacy impact assessment and possibly Canadian data residency.

## Per-pill location must come from the phone, and Vision alone may not be enough

Apple Vision has no pill detector, but iOS 17 provides the parts for a zero-training baseline. `VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` (iOS 17+) returns class-agnostic instance masks and can produce tight per-instance crops with the background removed ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vngenerateforegroundinstancemaskrequest.md); [VNInstanceMaskObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vninstancemaskobservation.md)). Apple documents no instance limit, no minimum object size and no hand behaviour, and calls it resource-intensive ([WWDC23 10176](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10176/)). The likely failure is that the hand and pills come back as one instance. A two-pass trick, cropping to the palm and lifting again, may help. That is the hypothesis the first experiment tests. The Swift-native `GenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` needs iOS 18, so an iOS 17 target means the `VN*` classes on a dedicated queue, or `#available` branches ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/generateforegroundinstancemaskrequest.md)). Saliency's 64×64 heat map is too coarse to separate touching pills ([VNSaliencyImageObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnsaliencyimageobservation.md)). Hand pose gives joints, not a mask, but a palm polygon from wrist and knuckle joints makes a useful region of interest ([VNDetectHumanHandPoseRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanhandposerequest.md)). Classical thresholding plus watershed works on uniform trays but breaks on skin. It also needs per-skin-tone tuning, which raises a fairness problem in a health app ([MathWorks thread](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/1592779-identify-objects-in-images)).

If subject lifting cannot separate the pills, train a **single-class "pill" detector**. One class keeps the detector independent of any patient's prescription. Ultralytics YOLO26n exports to Core ML with NMS embedded and runs detection in **3.7 ms and segmentation in 4.9 ms** (INT8, iPhone 17 Pro, Neural Engine) ([Ultralytics Core ML](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml/)). However, **Ultralytics is AGPL-3.0, and Ultralytics states the licence also covers trained weights**. Course use is free only if the whole app's source and weights are published under AGPL. A private repo, or any later commercial path, needs an Enterprise licence ([Ultralytics license](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)). Confirm with the course before choosing it. Two permissive options avoid this. **Create ML Object Detection** produces boxes only, and Apple says transfer learning "does well with as few as 80 training examples per class" ([Apple Tech Talk 10155](https://developer-rno.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/10155)). **RF-DETR** Nano–Large is Apache 2.0, but its Core ML export is experimental ([rf-detr](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr); [kortexa-ai Core ML port](https://huggingface.co/kortexa-ai/rf-detr-coreml)). Boxes are enough to crop and highlight. Running subject lifting inside each box then gives a clean mask at no licence cost. For pretraining, VAIPE includes in-hand pills ([PMC10538799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10538799/)), and MEDISEG is CC BY 4.0 with multi-pill occlusion ([arXiv 2603.10825](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10825)). Either way, about 100–300 self-collected palm photos are needed.

For the on-device embedding, **Vision feature print revision 2** (iOS 17+, 360×360 input, 768-D vector) is free and archivable through `NSSecureCoding` ([Apple revision docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/createmlcomponents/imagefeatureprint/revision.md); [VNFeaturePrintObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfeatureprintobservation.md)). Pin the revision, because distances between revisions fail with error 12 ([Dev Forums 732876](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/732876)). `computeDistance` has no documented scale, so any threshold has to be calibrated ([Dev Forums 721760](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/721760)). A generic embedding will probably separate a white round tablet from an orange capsule but not two white rounds that differ only by imprint. It is therefore a **rejection filter, not an identifier**: it can veto, but it cannot confirm alone. Cheap handcrafted cues from the mask strengthen it: mean Lab colour, ellipse aspect ratio, and size relative to the other pills. MobileCLIP2-S0 (11.4M-parameter image encoder) is the natural upgrade, with MIT code and Apple model terms to check ([apple/ml-mobileclip](https://github.com/apple/ml-mobileclip)). `VNRecognizeTextRequest` on an upscaled tight crop, with `customWords` set to the registered imprints, is a worthwhile extra imprint signal for printed imprints. Its default `minimumTextHeight` is 1/32 of image height ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnrecognizetextrequest/minimumtextheight.md)). Debossed imprints defeat conventional OCR ([GO-PILL, MDPI 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/356)), so OCR should only ever *add* a veto.

## Quality gating is cheap pixel maths on the pill region, run three times a second

The capture layer should run only inexpensive checks continuously, at the current ~3 Hz throttle, and save heavy work for the still. **Lighting** uses `exposureTargetOffset`, which is key-value observable, in EV units, and available since iOS 8 ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/exposuretargetoffset.md)). Add ISO and exposure duration, plus a 256-bin vImage histogram of the downscaled Y plane *within the pill and palm region*. A 3×3 grid of cell means catches uneven light, which auto-exposure hides. **Blur** needs three conditions: focus has settled (`isAdjustingFocus == false`, stable `lensPosition`); gyro rotation is low; and variance of Laplacian on the region is at least about 70% of its recent maximum, because absolute values depend on scene texture. Document scanners use the same idea, requiring N consecutive good frames before firing ([scannerpro](https://pub.dev/documentation/scannerpro/3.0.0/core_models_scanner_options/ScannerOptions-class.html); [docuSnap](https://github.com/petermartis/docuSnap)). Because the hand moves as well as the phone, **box IoU between analysed frames** is a necessary extra stability check ([Dynamsoft stabilizer](https://www.dynamsoft.com/codepool/android-document-scanner.html)). **Glare** is the fraction of each pill's area that is clipped and desaturated (Y ≥ ~245, near-neutral chroma). **Framing** checks come free from the detector's boxes: edge contact, pairwise overlap, pill size, and a stable count. Apple's ML quality requests do not fit as per-frame gates. Face-capture quality is face-only, aesthetics scores are iOS 18+ and blend memorability, and `DetectLensSmudgeRequest` is iOS 26+ and meant for stable stills, so use it only as an occasional "wipe the lens" check ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detectlenssmudgerequest.md)).

**Every number in this table is an untuned starting point from the notes, not a validated or clinical threshold.**

| Check (priority order for the single spoken hint) | Signal | Starting point to tune | Hint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermal | `thermalState` / `systemPressureState` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/systempressurestate-swift.class.md)) | `serious` → 1–2 Hz, torch off | "Phone is too warm" |
| Pills present | detector count | 0 | "Show the pills in your palm" |
| Dark / bright | ROI mean luma, clipped %, EV offset | <50 or >200 /255; >2–5% clipped | "Move to a brighter area" |
| Edge contact | box to border | <2% of frame | "Move pills to the centre" |
| Size | pill short side | <8–10% of frame short side | "Move closer" / "Move back" |
| Touching | box IoU or gap | IoU >0.05 | "Separate the pills" |
| Glare | clipped, desaturated pill pixels | 2–8% hint, >8% block | "Tilt to reduce glare" |
| Steady | gyro, VoL, box IoU | ω <0.15 rad/s; VoL ≥70% of recent max; IoU >0.9 | "Hold steady" |

Auto-capture is a small state machine: **searching → adjusting → steady (N≈3 consecutive good frames at 3 Hz, about 1 s) → capturing → validating**. When it reaches steady, lock focus and exposure and call `AVCapturePhotoOutput.capturePhoto` with `.balanced` prioritization ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturephotosettings/photoqualityprioritization.md)). Then re-run blur, glare and size checks on the still, with up to about three automatic retries before offering a manual shutter, which should always be available. Keep the torch off by default, because a close-range torch puts a centred reflection on glossy pills. Speak only *changes* in the single top hint, at most once every 2–3 s, using iOS 17's announcement priority ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/nsattributedstring/key/accessibilityspeechannouncementpriority.md)). Use a distinct haptic for "steady" and for success. Because pill colour depends on lighting hue ([JMIR 2021](https://medinform.jmir.org/2021/7/e26000)), consider a white-balance lock or a grey-world correction before colour cues are computed.

## Three architectures, ranked against the false-confirmation goal

| | **A. Hybrid gated (recommended)** | **B. All on-device** | **C. Cloud-first** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Localize | Device: subject lifting → single-class Create ML/RF-DETR detector if needed | Same | Whole photo to VLM; boxes from Gemini, or from OpenAI validated against device count |
| Identify | Feature-print kNN + ratio gate **AND** OpenAI closed-enum crop label **AND** imprint string check | Feature print/MobileCLIP + colour/shape + Vision OCR imprint | VLM label with structured output |
| False-confirm defence | Independent cues, AND gate | Single modality; weak on look-alike imprints | Correlated single model; verbalised confidence |
| Offline / privacy | Degrades to "cannot confirm" offline; crops leave device | Fully offline; nothing leaves device | Full palm photo leaves device |
| Cost | ~$0.01/scan | $0 | ~$0.007–0.02/scan |
| Main risk | Abstention rate too high | Cannot confirm white-round look-alikes | Mislocated red highlight; imprint hallucination |

**Architecture C** is the fastest to demo and the worst for safety. OpenAI cannot be trusted to place highlights, and one model's misread imprint becomes a green tick. **Architecture B** is the privacy ideal and should be built anyway as the veto layer. On its own, though, it will mostly answer "uncertain" for small white debossed tablets, the hardest category in every benchmark. **Architecture A** combines B's spatial certainty and distractor rejection with a cloud reader that is strongest where B is weakest, the imprint. The code-side imprint comparison closes the documented failure mode of a misread digit becoming a plausible wrong drug. A green result requires every check to pass, so each added check can only lower false confirmations.

### Repo mapping: what is reused and what is replaced

`Camera/CameraSession` is **kept and extended**. Add an `AVCapturePhotoOutput`, focus and exposure lock, and torch control. Replace the hard-wired `VisionTextRecognizer` with an injected frame analyzer, so the existing `AsyncStream` (buffering newest 1) carries `FrameQuality` instead of `RecognizedFrame`. `Camera/VisionTextRecognizer` stays as the label-scanning path and as the template for a new `PillImprintReader`. `Core/DoseSchedule` is **reused unchanged** to compute the doses due now, and it becomes the source of the expected set. `Core/MedicationMatcher`'s token and strength normalisation is reused for comparing imprint strings, but its name matching does not apply to pills. `Core/ScanOutcome` is a single-medication label result. It is **kept for label scans and supplemented** by a per-pill and set-level result, rather than overloaded. `Data/MedicationStore` gains products, appearances and enrolled reference feature prints, through its existing storage seam. `Features/Scan/ScanFlow` keeps session state, undo and auto-close, but auto-closes only on `.verified` and marks all due doses taken as a single undoable action. `TextBoxOverlay` becomes a `PillOverlay`. `DesignSystem/Palette` needs a "do not take" red and a "cannot confirm" state that also carries an icon and text, so it does not rely on colour alone. The new modules are `Capture/` (quality analyzer, auto-capture state machine), `PillVision/` (localizer protocol with SubjectLift and CoreML implementations, embedder, imprint reader), `Identification/` (the remote identifier client for the proxy, and `PillVerifier`, the pure-Swift gate), and `Verification/` (`SetVerifier`, also pure Swift and unit-testable with mock data).

```swift
/// Medication → dispensed product (DIN) → physical appearance(s).
nonisolated struct DispensedProduct: Hashable, Sendable { let din: String; let manufacturer: String; let appearances: [PillAppearance] }
nonisolated struct PillAppearance: Hashable, Sendable {
    let id: String; let color: String; let shape: PillShape
    let imprintFront: String?; let imprintBack: String?; let sizeMM: Double?
}

nonisolated struct PillLocation: Hashable, Sendable {
    let id: String                  // "pill_1"… stable for this scan, sent to the proxy
    let boundingBox: CGRect         // normalized, image space of the captured still
    let maskInstance: Int?          // index into the in-memory instance mask, if any
}

nonisolated struct IdentityCandidate: Hashable, Sendable {
    let medicationID: String
    let embeddingDistance: Float    // d1 for this med; lower is closer (uncalibrated scale)
    let ratioToRunnerUp: Float      // d1 / d2
    let cloudLabel: CloudLabel?     // .medication(id) | .none | .unknown | .refused
    let imprintRead: String?        // nil when illegible, never a guess
    let imprintAgrees: Bool?        // code-side comparison to registered imprint
}

nonisolated enum PillVerdict: Hashable, Sendable {
    case confirmed(medicationID: String)           // every gate passed
    case notMine                                   // far from every enrolled reference
    case uncertain(reasons: Set<UncertaintyReason>)// any gate failed or disagreed
}
nonisolated enum UncertaintyReason: Hashable, Sendable {
    case lowSimilarity, ambiguousLookAlike, recognizersDisagree,
         imprintMismatch, imprintIllegible, poorCrop, cloudUnavailable
}

nonisolated struct PillResult: Identifiable, Hashable, Sendable {
    let location: PillLocation
    let candidates: [IdentityCandidate]  // sorted best-first, only registered meds
    let verdict: PillVerdict
    let mustNotTake: Bool                // red: notMine, uncertain, or confirmed-but-not-due
    var id: String { location.id }
}

nonisolated enum SetVerdict: Hashable, Sendable {
    case verified                                  // all confirmed, multiset == due now
    case mismatch(extra: [String], missing: [Dose])// some pill must not be taken / a due med absent
    case uncertain                                 // anything else; never shown as green
}
nonisolated struct PalmScanResult: Sendable { let pills: [PillResult]; let verdict: SetVerdict; let scannedAt: Date }
```

`SetVerifier` returns `.verified` only when every pill is `.confirmed` and the multiset of confirmed IDs equals the due-now multiset from `DoseSchedule`, including quantity. It returns `.mismatch` when any pill is `notMine` or confirmed but not due, or when a due medication is absent. Every other case is `.uncertain`. **A pill the app cannot confirm is treated as "do not take yet" and gets a red outline with a "can't confirm, check the bottle" label**. It is never green and never silently ignored. A detected-count change between the last steady frame and the still also forces `.uncertain`, because a missed wrong pill is a hidden false confirmation. All thresholds inside `PillVerifier` (τ_abs, τ_ratio, agreement count) are tuneable starting points, not clinical values.

**Reference data.** Model the medical-record story at DIN level. Health Canada's Drug Product Database API is free under the Open Government Licence – Canada and returns DIN, brand, ingredient, strength, form and company, with monthly bulk extracts ([DPD API](https://health-products.canada.ca/api/documentation/dpd-documentation-en.html); [open.canada.ca](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/bf55e42a-63cb-4556-bfd8-44f26e5a36fe)). It has **no colour, shape, imprint or image fields**. Appearance exists only as prose in the sponsor's product monograph PDF, linked from each DPD page ([example DPD page](https://health-products.canada.ca/dpd-bdpp/info?lang=eng&code=73825)). For about 5–20 mock DINs, transcribe appearance by hand into a schema modelled on US SPL characteristics (SPLCOLOR, SPLSHAPE, SPLIMPRINT) ([HL7 value set](https://terminology.hl7.org/ValueSet-v3-MedicationObservationType.html)). Allow several appearances per DIN, because generic substitution changes appearance between refills. US Pillbox and RxImage are retired and NLM says not to use them for identification ([NLM bulletin](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/ja20/ja20_pillbox_discontinue.html)). Canadian pill photos exist only in commercial products such as Vigilance Santé and CPS ([Vigilance](https://www.vigilance.ca/drug-database-photo)). So the enrolment photos must be self-collected from the demo pills: placebo or vitamin look-alikes, or the team's own OTC products.

### Evaluation centred on false confirmation

The headline metric is the **false confirmation rate**: pills shown as OK whose true identity differs from the claim. Report it per stratum (registered nominal, other strength of a registered drug, look-alike, unregistered distractor, adverse conditions), each with an exact Clopper–Pearson upper bound. Next to it, report the abstention rate, a risk–coverage curve, a scan-level false-OK rate (the scan said "all good" but at least one pill was wrong), detection recall, and red-highlight sensitivity. By the rule of three, **zero errors in N trials only supports a rate ≤3/N at 95% confidence** ([Hanley & Lippman-Hand, JAMA 1983](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/389714)). Supporting ≤5% needs about 60 clean distractor or look-alike trials, and ≤1% needs about 300. The report should state this plainly. Split by physical pill and capture session, never by photo. Use three splits (enrolment, calibration, test), lock thresholds before the test run, keep some distractor products unseen during calibration, and log the model version and date for every cloud call. Measure P(OpenAI wrong | embedding wrong) directly, to show whether the AND gate actually adds independence. Frame the work as early **analytical validation** in IMDRF terms. Clinical validation, meaning real patients, real regimens and observed wrong-pill events, is out of scope ([IMDRF N41](https://www.imdrf.org/docs/imdrf/final/technical/imdrf-tech-170921-samd-n41-clinical-evaluation_1.pdf)). Do not claim any accuracy threshold as "safe", because none exists in the literature.

### The first experiment: localization and rejection feasibility in one week

Choose 4 mock medications, including one white round tablet and one same-drug different-strength pair if obtainable, plus 6 distractors (acetaminophen, ibuprofen, a vitamin, a mint, and two other white rounds). Enrol 5 shots per side per medication. Then capture **40 palm scans of 4 pills** across 3 lighting conditions, at least 3 team members' hands, and 2 iPhones, in a separate session from enrolment. Roughly half the scans should include at least one distractor or wrong-strength pill. In a debug build that keeps images in memory only, run three measurements. (1) **Localization:** run `VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest` on the full still and on a palm crop, and record exact-count rate and per-pill box IoU against hand labels. If exact-count is poor, which is likely, that alone justifies training the Create ML single-class detector next. (2) **On-device rejection:** compute feature-print revision 2 distances and plot distractor *d1* against genuine *d1*. (3) **OpenAI A/B:** send the same crops through the proxy to `gpt-6.1-terra` and `gpt-6.1-sol`, with and without reference images, using the closed-enum schema. Record false-confident labels, imprint read accuracy and real `usage` tokens. About 160 crops × 4 variants costs a few dollars. The go/no-go question is narrow: at the threshold where **zero of the distractor and wrong-strength crops pass the full AND gate**, what fraction of genuine pills are still confirmed? With only about 80 adversarial crops, a zero result supports a false-confirm rate of no more than about 4% at 95% confidence, not "safe". If that coverage is usable, Architecture A is viable. If not, the data shows which gate to strengthen: localization, enrolment shots or imprint reading.

## Conclusion

The central design choice is not which model to call. It is to separate *where* from *what*, and *what* from *whether to say yes*. Location belongs on the device because a misplaced red box is itself a safety failure. Identity can come from cheap, swappable cloud reads, because the closed medication list collapses the open-world problem. The decision to confirm belongs in a small, deterministic, unit-tested Swift gate that any single component can veto. That makes the $80 OpenAI budget nearly irrelevant to the result, and it makes the abstention rate the metric that decides whether the product is usable. The team can measure that rate in its first week.

Two contributions from this project would be new to the published record: a measured false-accept rate for out-of-set pills against a small enrolled set, and the first in-palm versus flat-surface comparison. Both fall out of the first experiment almost for free. The weakest assumption in the story is not the AI but the data. Confirming a pill needs the *dispensed DIN's* appearance, and in Canada that exists only as monograph prose or behind commercial licences. A real deployment would need appearance and reference photos registered at the point of dispensing, which makes the pharmacy workflow, not the model, the safety-critical integration.
